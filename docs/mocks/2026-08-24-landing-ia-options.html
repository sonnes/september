Landing page redesign: information architecture

Structure only — section order, what each section carries, and what is new, merged, or cut. Copy in the blocks is directional, not final. Visual design (type, color, art direction) comes in a second mock after one structure is chosen. All three options keep the page's strongest asset: every demo on the page is the real feature, working.

What the current page does, and where it strains

Today: hero → six feature demos of equal weight → about → privacy → setup → CTA → footer.

Why the demo moves into the hero

The strongest claim the page can make is not a headline — it is the product working in the first viewport. The current page already puts the demo second; fusing it with the hero removes one scroll and makes the promise ("fewer keystrokes") and its proof one gesture apart.

Six features → four chapters

Talk is the hero, so it stops being a chapter. Notes and Present collapse into one arc (the Present demo already replays the Notes sentences). Each remaining chapter keeps its color lane and its working demo, alternating text-left / text-right as today.

The new platform section (A6)

The single biggest content gap. Two calm columns, no screenshots needed at IA stage: browser = start now; Mac = calls and on-device writing help. Decision needed on the Mac column's action — "Download for Mac" if a bundle will be published, otherwise an honest "The Mac app is on its way" with a link to GitHub.

Privacy absorbs "free" (A7)

One indigo band answers the two anxieties that arrive together — "where do my words go?" and "what does it cost?" — and Setup (A9) stops re-stating them, which was the current overlap.

Nav

Hero-card nav grows to Features · Calls · Privacy · About + Get started. No sticky header — calm wins; the footer keeps the same anchors for the way back up.

ForLowest risk and least new content; every existing demo is reused; fixes all six audit findings.
AgainstStill a feature-shaped page — the emotional register lives only in the About section and the chapter ledes.

Why a day

The audience does not shop for features — they picture tomorrow morning. A day-shaped page lets each demo answer a concrete moment ("breakfast", "the clinic", "the call", "the story") instead of a category name. The founder's article is written in exactly this register, so the page and the story land in one voice.

What it costs

Every scene needs new framing copy, and each demo needs a scene-specific seed (morning phrases, clinic phrases — partly existing). Feature names (Spaces, Notes) become secondary, which is worse for the clinician skimming for AAC capabilities, and worse for anchor navigation.

The call scene earns its place

In a day-shaped page the Mac app is not an appendix — it is the noon scene. This is the structure that markets the virtual microphone and camera hardest.

ForMost emotionally persuasive; the Mac app gets a starring scene; one narrative thread from hero to About.
AgainstMost new copy; feature findability drops for evaluators; scenes date faster than chapters as features change.

Why questions

The audience arrives scared and skimming. Question headers let a reader self-serve: they scroll until they see their own worry in 30px type, and read only that answer. It is also the friendliest structure for a caregiver forwarding the page — "scroll to the third question".

The trade

Headers stop naming features, so the page markets reassurance more than capability. Nine question bands in a row also risk monotony — the rhythm depends entirely on the answers alternating demo / prose well. The closing merge (C9) removes the dark CTA band, which weakens the final beat.

ForMatches the reader's emotional state; extremely scannable; caregiver question gets first-class placement.
AgainstFeature names vanish from the skeleton; weakest closing CTA; headline tone can tip from calm into clinical if the questions read as a symptom list.
